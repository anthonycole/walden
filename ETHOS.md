# Walden

Walden is an example of what software in the Strata industry _could_ be. It is inspired by the writing of Stewart Brand about [maintenance](https://books.worksinprogress.co/book/maintenance-of-everything/addenda/page/introduction) and Paul Graham's essay on [good taste](https://paulgraham.com/goodtaste.html)

When you buy into an apartment or townhouse in Australia, you’re basically buying into a small, medium, or large sized company with a quantiative amount of stakeholders and business units. Each one needs to pass a budget, possibly file a tax return and deal with varying levels of customer complaints and legislation. A manager usually assists you and they come from what is known as the _strata_, _body corporate_, or other industry.

My philosophy with software has always been “I don’t want to think about it”. But I feel that strata software is the opposite - most of it is designed for function over form. Most of it does too much. And that’s a problem because it stifles cohesive owner engagement.

Most software in the industry isn't owner-centric - it feels like you’re in the 90's again.

What this software did for me was the following:
* Helped identify that a former manager of ours had mishandled the funds we had.
* Alerted me, visually, to a somewhat high utility bill. 
* Showed me trends and allowed me to navigate, communicate, and understand what our building was spending on.

I'm still figuring this out as I go, but a future for this might be:
* UX Patterns and Open Standards for portability between strata software systemss
* Agentic tooling for understanding strata schemes better
* Open source documentation for meeting minutes and general committee business.

It is named after the [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden](book) by Henry David Thoreau.
